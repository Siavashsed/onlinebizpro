## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do you build a personal brand that actually attracts clients and investors?

Having built and sold two companies and advised a number of founders since, I'd say most people approach this backwards. They start with the visuals: logo, color palette, professional headshot. Those are decoration. A personal brand that attracts clients and investors does one job: a stranger finds you, understands in about ten seconds what you fix, and sees proof that you've fixed it before.

Here's how I'd build it.

1. Name the problem you fix. Not your job title. 'I help B2B SaaS founders cut sales cycles' beats 'serial entrepreneur and thought leader' every time. Specificity is what makes someone remember you.

2. Put the proof where strangers land. Your LinkedIn headline, your About section and your website should show concrete results: numbers, named outcomes, short case notes. Claims are cheap. Evidence is what builds trust.

3. Publish how you think. Write about real problems you've solved, including what went wrong. Investors and clients are evaluating your judgment, not just your résumé.

4. Be consistent over a long horizon. Two weeks before I sold my second company, an acquirer's associate sent me a printout of a LinkedIn post I had written over a year earlier. It was plain, specific and honest, and it shaped their confidence more than anything polished I could have produced on short notice.

5. Make it easy to verify. Link to results, testimonials, press or data. The easier you are to check, the more trustworthy you appear.

The test I give founders: show your profile to a stranger for ten seconds, then ask what you do and why they should believe it. If they can't answer, no amount of design will fix it.
