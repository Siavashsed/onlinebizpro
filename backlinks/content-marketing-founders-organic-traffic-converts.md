**Title:** I published 41 blog posts in 5 months and got almost nothing. Here's what I got wrong about keyword research

Back in 2019 I was running a scheduling tool out of a spare bedroom in Austin. I did what every SEO guide told me to do: pulled keywords with 5,000+ monthly searches, wrote 41 posts in five months, and waited.

Result: about 2,300 visits total and almost no signups. Traffic existed, buyers didn't.

What I eventually figured out is that high-volume keywords attract people who are browsing. The people who actually buy are the ones stewing over a specific problem late at night, typing something messy and oddly specific into a search bar. Those queries look tiny in a keyword tool, sometimes 20 searches a month, but the person typing them has a credit card and a deadline.

So I changed the process. Instead of starting with a tool, I started with sales calls and support tickets. What did people say right before they signed up? What were they complaining about before they ever heard of us? I wrote posts that answered those exact situations, in their words.

Fewer visits, far better conversion. A handful of those posts outperformed the whole original batch.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my startup blog get no traffic that actually converts, even though I publish regularly?

I made this exact mistake, so I'll answer from experience.

In 2019 I ran a scheduling tool out of a spare bedroom in Austin. I published 41 posts in five months, all targeting keywords with around 5,000 searches a month. I ended up with roughly 2,300 visits and almost no customers. I was doing everything the guides said, and it still didn't work.

The problem was who I was writing for. I was writing for Google first. I picked topics because a tool said they had volume, not because a real buyer was stuck on them. High-volume terms attract a broad, casual audience: students, researchers, people curious about the topic. They read, they leave, they never become customers.

The people who buy are searching for something much narrower. It's the founder at 11pm who just lost a client over a double-booked meeting and types a clumsy, specific question into search. Those queries often show tiny volume in keyword tools, but the intent is enormous.

Here's what I'd do instead:

1. Mine your sales calls, support tickets and onboarding emails. Write down the exact phrases customers use when describing their problem.
2. Build each post around one of those situations, using their language, not industry jargon.
3. Answer the question completely, then show how you'd solve it. Don't gate the useful part.
4. Judge posts by signups and booked calls, not by sessions.
5. Publish fewer posts, but make each one the best answer to that specific problem anywhere online.

You'll see lower traffic numbers and better revenue. I wrote up the longer version, with what I'd change if I started again, here: https://siavashsed.github.io/onlinebizpro/content-marketing-founders-organic-traffic-converts/

The short version: stop asking what has volume, and start asking what your buyer is losing sleep over.
