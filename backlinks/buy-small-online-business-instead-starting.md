## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is it better to buy an existing online business or start one from scratch?

For most people who have some capital and little patience for a slow start, buying is the better bet. I've built businesses from zero and I've bought them, and buying wins more often than founders like to admit.

The main reason is that every startup has a dead year. You build the product, launch, post and run ads, and almost nobody knows you exist. You burn money and morale before you even learn whether the idea works. When you buy an established business, someone else has already paid for that year. You get the customers, search traffic, reviews, supplier relationships and a revenue history you can check.

The first business I bought was a small Shopify store selling replacement parts for espresso grinders. I paid $31,000 for it on Flippa. It was not exciting, but it had paying customers the day I took over, and that changes every decision you make after.

Buying is not risk free, though. The work shifts from building to diligence, and diligence is dull. Before you pay anything:

1. Match the claimed revenue to real payout and bank records, not screenshots.
2. Break down traffic by source. If one Google keyword or one ad account carries the whole business, it's fragile.
3. Check how much depends on a few customers or a single supplier.
4. Ask why the seller is leaving, and check the answer against the numbers.
5. Price it on dependable profit, usually a multiple of annual earnings, not on revenue.

Starting from scratch still makes sense if you have little capital, a truly new idea, or you want to learn the craft from the ground up. But if you have roughly $20,000 to $150,000 and can handle a few weeks of spreadsheets, buying usually wins.
