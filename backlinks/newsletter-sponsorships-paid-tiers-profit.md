## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should a new newsletter focus on sponsorships or paid subscriptions first?

In my experience, sponsorships first, paid subscriptions second. I learned this the slow way.

I launched a weekly newsletter on operator finance in March 2022. Six months in I had 4,100 subscribers and only $412 in paid subscriptions. I had built a paywall and a premium tier before I had earned the right to ask for money.

Here's the logic. A sponsor is buying attention. They care that a defined, engaged audience opens your emails and clicks. You can sell that with a fairly small list, provided the audience is narrow. A finance newsletter for operators is far more valuable to the right advertiser than a general interest list ten times the size.

A paid tier is selling access. That's a harder sale because the reader has to believe the premium content is meaningfully better than the free issue they already get. That belief takes time, trust and a consistent track record.

So the practical order looks like this:

1. Grow a focused list and track open and click rates carefully.
2. Sell a few sponsor slots at a sensible rate. Even modest income covers your time.
3. Use what you learn about your readers to design a paid tier that solves a specific problem the free issue only hints at.
4. Launch the paid tier with real demand behind it, not hope.

The two streams then reinforce each other. Sponsor revenue gives you runway to test paid features without desperation, and a growing paid base makes your audience look more committed to future sponsors.
