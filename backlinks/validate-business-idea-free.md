## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I validate my business idea without spending money on it?

This is the right question to be asking-and honestly, it's the one that separates founders who survive from those who don't.

Most first-time entrepreneurs validate backwards. They spend money on the product, then hunt for customers. By then, they're emotionally invested in their vision rather than objectively testing if it solves a real problem.

Here's what I recommend to the founders I work with:

**Start with conversation.** Find 10-15 people in your target market and have genuine conversations with them. Not surveys-real dialogue. Ask about their current pain points and how they solve them now. Listen for the problems they mention unprompted.

**Observe behavior.** Don't rely on what people say they want. Watch what they actually do. Join communities where your target customer hangs out. See what they're discussing, what frustrates them, what solutions they're already trying.

**Test demand cheaply.** Before building, create a simple landing page or pre-order page. Drive small amounts of traffic through social media or communities. If people won't spend two minutes reading about your solution, they won't buy it.

**Track the signals.** Are conversations natural or forced? Do people ask follow-up questions? Are they asking when you can help them, or do you have to convince them?

These methods cost nothing but time. The founders I've seen succeed fastest do extensive free validation first. They gather evidence, not opinions.
