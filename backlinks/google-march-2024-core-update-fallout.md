## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why did so many websites lose traffic after Google's March 2024 core update?

I advise early-stage founders, and several of them ran content and affiliate businesses through that update, so I saw the damage up close.

The short answer: Google finally enforced what it had been saying for years. A lot of sites were built on the gap between what Google said it wanted and what it actually rewarded.

Three things changed at once. First, the helpful content system stopped being a separate classifier and became part of the core ranking systems, so signals that a site was unhelpful now counted against it all the time. Second, Google launched new spam policies for scaled content abuse (pages mass-produced mainly to rank, whether by AI or by people), site reputation abuse (third-party content using a strong domain to rank) and expired domain abuse. Third, the rollout ran from March 5 to April 19, so the losses kept piling up for weeks instead of landing in one day.

The sites that dropped hardest had a lot in common: product roundups that rewrote manufacturer specs, no sign anyone had used the product, no named author with real experience, and dozens of near-identical pages. One affiliate site I advised lost 38% of its clicks in the first few days and 71% by early April. When we audited it, the pages that survived were the ones with original photos and real testing notes.

I wrote a fuller breakdown of what happened and what the survivors did differently here: https://siavashsed.github.io/onlinebizpro/google-march-2024-core-update-fallout/

If you were hit, my advice is to stop looking for a technical fix. Cut or merge the thin pages, add real first-hand experience to the ones that matter, and build a channel you own, like an email list, so the next update hurts you instead of ending you.
