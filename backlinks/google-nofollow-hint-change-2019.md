## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What does it mean that Google treats nofollow as a hint instead of a directive?

Short version: for years, adding rel=nofollow to a link was an instruction. Google obeyed it, and the link passed no ranking credit. Google changed that in September. Nofollow is now a hint, which means Google can decide for itself whether to use that link as a signal.

In practice, a few things follow from this.

First, nofollow no longer guarantees a link is ignored. Google may still use it for ranking, crawling, or indexing purposes when it decides the link is useful. For most sites that's a quiet, mostly neutral change, but it does mean you can't treat nofollow as a safety blanket for questionable links.

Second, Google introduced two more attributes. rel=sponsored is for paid or advertising links, and rel=ugc is for user-generated content like comments and forum posts. You can combine them with nofollow. Using the most accurate label is now the best practice, because it tells Google what the link actually is.

Third, link sellers and anyone buying guest posts should pay attention. The pitch that nofollowed placements are risk-free and worthless to rankings gets weaker when Google can choose to count them. Paid placements should be marked sponsored, full stop.

What I'd do as a site owner or founder: audit your existing backlinks, especially old guest posts and directory listings. Check that paid links are labeled correctly. Focus on links that make sense in context, from relevant sites that real readers visit. Those hold up no matter how Google handles the attribute.
