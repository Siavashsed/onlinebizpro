**Title:** Your freelancer invoices might be showing you a hiring decision you keep putting off

In 2019 I was running a coffee subscription business out of a rented unit in Columbus. Over 11 months I paid three different freelance designers. Each one was good. Each one also spent the first few weeks learning our brand, our packaging limits, and the ideas we had already tried and dropped. I was paying for that ramp-up three times and calling it flexibility.

What I eventually worked out is that revenue and headcount aren't the signal to hire full-time. The signal is work that never stops and depends on context that takes months to build. If you keep explaining the same background to every new contractor, you're paying for it again on every invoice.

Freelancers are still the right call for work with a clear start and end, or for a specialist you only need a few times a year.

My rule now is to read invoices like a report. If the same kind of work shows up every month and onboarding time keeps showing up with it, you've probably waited too long.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I know when it's time to hire a full-time employee instead of using freelancers?

I'm Ryan Cross. I've started a few companies and now advise founders, and I once got this question wrong in an expensive way.

The short answer is to hire full-time when the work never stops and depends on context that takes months to build. Give the rest to freelancers.

Both conditions have to be true.

Continuous work. If you need design, bookkeeping or content every week with no natural end, you aren't buying a project. You're buying a function, and functions belong with people who stay.

Accumulated context. Some work can go to anyone competent who has a good brief. Other work only gets good after someone has learned your customers, your past mistakes and your constraints. Every time a freelancer leaves, that knowledge leaves with them, and the next one starts from zero.

In 2019 I ran a coffee subscription business out of a rented unit in Columbus. Over 11 months I went through three freelance designers. None of them were bad. But I paid each one to learn the brand, the packaging specs and the ideas we had already killed. I never budgeted for that onboarding because it was buried inside normal invoices.

That gives you a practical test: read your invoices. Look for the same kind of work showing up month after month. Look for hours spent on ramp-up, on revisions caused by missing background, or on re-explaining things. When you see that pattern, you've probably already waited too long.

Freelancers are still the better fit for defined projects, for specialist skills you need a few times a year, and for experiments you aren't sure will last.

I wrote a longer breakdown of how to read those signals here, if it helps: https://siavashsed.github.io/onlinebizpro/when-to-hire-vs-use-freelancers/

If you're on the fence, count how many times you've onboarded someone for the same work. Two is a warning. Three is your answer.
