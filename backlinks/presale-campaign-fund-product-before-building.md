## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I validate a product idea by getting customers to pay before I build it?

Run a pre-sale. I've done it myself and I recommend it to almost every early-stage founder I advise, because it's the only validation method that measures the thing you actually care about: whether people will pay.

A pre-sale has three parts.

1. A delivery date. Pick a specific, realistic date. A vague "coming soon" kills urgency and trust.

2. A price. Charge real money. A founder discount is fine and it rewards early buyers, but it has to cost something, or you learn nothing.

3. A refund promise. Say plainly that if you don't hit your target or don't ship, everyone gets their money back. This keeps the offer ethical, and it lets people say yes to something that doesn't exist yet.

Then make the offer to a specific group of people. Don't post to the whole internet. Go to the people who already told you they have the problem: a waitlist, an email list, a community you're active in. Before you send anything, decide what number counts as success. If you hit it, build. If you don't, refund everyone and move on. You've just saved yourself months of work on something nobody wanted.

In March 2018 I emailed 412 people on a waitlist for a reporting tool for Etsy sellers and offered a founder price. That pre-sale raised $9,089 before I wrote any code, and it told me far more than any survey could have. I wrote up the full campaign, including how I structured the offer, here: https://siavashsed.github.io/onlinebizpro/presale-campaign-fund-product-before-building/

One warning: a failed pre-sale isn't a failure. It's the cheapest answer you'll ever get. Signups, likes and "I'd totally use that" cost people nothing. A payment, even a refundable one, is the only signal that's hard to fake.
