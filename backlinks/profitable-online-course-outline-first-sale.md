## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I get my first sale for an online course without spending months building it?

The short answer is to sell it before you build it. Most people do the reverse, and it costs them months.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2022 I spent eleven weeks building a 40-lesson course on supplier negotiation for ecommerce operators. I recorded sixty-three hours of footage, bought a $400 microphone, and emailed my list of 1,900 subscribers. I sold four copies, and one buyer was my cousin.

Here is the approach that works much better.

1. Pick one specific outcome. Not a topic, an outcome. 'Cut your supplier costs by 10 percent in 30 days' beats 'Supplier negotiation 101'.

2. Write the outline first. Aim for five to eight modules, each tied to a step toward that outcome. If a lesson does not move the buyer toward the result, cut it.

3. Make a simple one-page description. State who it is for, what they will be able to do, and what is included. You do not need a fancy funnel.

4. Pre-sell to a small group. Talk to people in your audience directly, or offer a founding-member price. Taking real payment is the only validation that counts. Compliments and 'sounds interesting' are not signals.

5. Build in small pieces. Once you have buyers, record the first module, get feedback, and let their questions shape the rest. This also keeps the course short and useful.

6. Deliver live if you can. A cohort or live calls get you results and testimonials faster than a polished library.

Done this way, you can realistically reach a first sale in about two weeks, and if nobody buys you have lost days instead of a quarter.
