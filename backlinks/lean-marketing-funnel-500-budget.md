## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should I spend a $500 monthly marketing budget for my small business?

The short answer: spend it on one funnel, not several.

I learned this the expensive way. In March 2019 I put $500 into Facebook ads for a small cast iron seasoning brand I was running out of a rented garage in Tulsa. I split it across four ad sets, a lookalike audience and a retargeting campaign. Each got a tiny daily budget, none collected enough data to optimize, and I came away with very little I could act on.

Here's the approach I'd take now, and the one I give founders I advise:

1. Pick one offer. Choose the product or service with the clearest value and the best margin. Don't promote your whole catalog.

2. Pick one traffic source. Paid social, search ads, or a single organic channel you're already good at. Whichever you choose, put the whole budget there so it can gather enough data to show you what works.

3. Build one follow-up sequence. Most people don't buy on the first visit. A simple email sequence of a few messages recovers a lot of the leads you'd otherwise lose, and it costs very little compared to extra ad spend.

4. Fund nothing else until this path produces a result. Lookalike audiences, retargeting and multiple creatives are useful, but they need volume to pay off. At $500 a month you don't have that volume yet.

5. Review monthly. Look at cost per lead and cost per sale. If the numbers hold up, add the next layer. If they don't, change the offer or the message before touching the budget.

The mindset shift is that a small budget isn't a small version of a big funnel. It's a test that should give you one clear answer.
