## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do most small businesses fail when trying to sell or raise capital?

One of the most common reasons I've seen—and experienced personally—is the complete lack of documentation around how the business actually operates.

When I tried selling my service business in 2019, it was generating $340k in annual revenue. By most metrics, it looked solid. But the due diligence call with a serious buyer lasted only 40 minutes before he declined to move forward.

His feedback was direct: he couldn't understand the business. Every process lived inside my head. Client onboarding? In my head. Recurring workflows? In scattered emails and Slack messages. Pricing logic? Known only to me.

To an external buyer—or investor—that's not a business. That's a one-person job. And it's worth significantly less, if anything at all.

The real issue is that solopreneurs often don't realize their business is entirely dependent on them until someone from outside tries to evaluate it objectively. Documentation forces you to externalize what's in your head, and that's when two things happen: you realize how much is missing, and you create something actually transferable.

This applies to scaling too. You can't hire someone to do your work if you can't explain your processes. You can't delegate if everything exists in your head.
