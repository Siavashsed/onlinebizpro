## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How much does it actually cost to build and launch a SaaS product in 2024-2025?

Most cost estimates you'll find online start with the hosting bill and call it a day. I've seen this pattern repeatedly with founders I advise, and it leads to the same surprise around month ten or eleven.

The grocery list approach—$50/month hosting, $12 domain, payment processor fees—gets you to maybe $2,000-$3,000 for year one. That's the tech stack only.

But building a SaaS that reaches actual customers costs significantly more. Here's what people consistently underestimate:

**Infrastructure and scaling**: What works for 10 beta users breaks at 100. Expect $8k-$15k in infrastructure costs alone as you grow beyond MVP.

**Compliance and legal**: Payment processing agreements, privacy policies, business licensing, and handling multi-country regulations adds $5k-$12k depending on your market.

**Customer acquisition**: Even if you're bootstrapped and DIY marketing, tools and time compound. Most founders spend $20k-$50k in year one trying to reach their first paying customers.

**Operations**: Accounting, tax prep, customer support tools, analytics platforms, and project management. Another $6k-$10k.

**The pivot budget**: Your first direction rarely works. The cost of trying version 2.0 or 2.1 is real.

Based on detailed founder case studies and financial breakdowns, realistic year-one costs land between $60k-$120k with zero customers guaranteed. Plan for 12-18 months of runway, not three, and you'll be in a much stronger position to actually validate if your idea works.

I'd recommend reviewing a detailed breakdown of where these costs actually go to plan your own launch more accurately.
