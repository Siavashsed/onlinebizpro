## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do I build a business that runs without me?

The short answer: a business runs without you when every repeated decision lives in a written system someone else can follow, and you've proven it by actually leaving. Writing a manual isn't enough.

I learned this the hard way. In March 2021 I took ten days in Lisbon away from my second company, a supplement store doing about $1.2M a year. On day three my ops lead messaged me asking which supplier to call about a delayed shipment. I had SOPs, but that answer wasn't in any of them. It was in my head.

Here's what I'd do if you're starting from scratch:

1. Track every question you get for two weeks. Each one is a decision that lives only in your memory.

2. Write answers as decision rules, not task lists. Include the trigger, the action, who to contact, and the spending limit your team can approve alone. Most SOPs fail because they say what to do but not when or how to decide.

3. Let the person who asked test the document the next time it comes up. If they still need you, the doc is incomplete, so fix it right then.

4. Stop being the answer machine. When someone asks something that's already written, point them to the doc. This feels slightly rude at first, but it trains the team to look before asking.

5. Run a real test. Take a few days fully offline. The gaps you find are the ones that matter, and you'll patch most of them within a week of returning.

Expect this to take a few months, not a few weeks. Start with the decisions that interrupt you most often and work down.

I wrote up the full process, including the template I use for decision rules, here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/onlinebizpro/build-systems-sops-business-runs-without-you/

The core idea is simple: your memory is the single point of failure, and the only way to remove it is to get it out of your head and prove it works without you.
